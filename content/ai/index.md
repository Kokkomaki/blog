I use AI to code, research, brainstorm, assess, analyse, paraphrase, experiment, and chat with. Haters can hate, but in my experience AI will shift the landscape as we know it.

Absolutists and maximalists of the opposite kind will certainly disagree with the ethics, sustainability, and potential dangers of AI. But AI itself will accelerate and create a lot of synergy, creativity, and experimentation in many fields.

As a writer, I find AI a tremendous tool, though it has its grave limitations. Certainly, because it is my personal blog, I have a lot of pride in my writing, and contend that I am sufficiently good at writing. Still, I find myself occasionally aided by AI in my writing, though I notice its average soul from the paragraphs, to sentences to the words and special marks, like the infamous em-dash.

And yet, those who did not learn to Google, and who will not learn AI, even with its mistakes, will not understand how to use AI in the future. At the same time, it is crucial that we understand first how to write, before we let AI write; that we understand how to create proper code architectures, before we entrust ourselves with security responsibilities in software engineering.

I wrote this blog with AI, my server, my newsletter, my analytics, a website debankd.org, and much more. It is a phenomenal tool, and I find that I am able to express my creativity in ways that I could not have before. It lets me create business ideas, strategies and explain everything like I am a toddler.

So no: utilising AI is part of me, as much as smartphones are. However, at the end of the day: first comes experience, then storytelling. If I am not able to tell the story in my own words, with the help of AI, I wasn't a great storyteller to begin with.
